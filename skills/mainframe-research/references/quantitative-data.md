# Quantitative-data research

Apply this reference to statistics, prices, rates, currencies, money, ratios, surveys, forecasts, benchmarks, market estimates, and calculated comparisons.

## Define the measure before comparing it

Establish the producer, dataset and series, geography, covered population, reference period, release vintage, frequency, unit, scale, currency, price basis, seasonal-adjustment status, and whether the value is a level, index, flow, stock, rate, share, or change.

Keep these distinctions explicit when relevant:

- nominal versus real, including the base year or deflator;
- seasonally adjusted versus unadjusted;
- total versus per-capita and aggregate versus component values;
- current-price versus constant-price values;
- percentage change versus percentage-point change;
- preliminary, revised, benchmarked, forecast, and final values;
- calendar, fiscal, rolling, point-in-time, and year-over-year periods.

## Prefer the producing source

Use the producing statistical agency, central bank, regulator, exchange, company filing, or dataset owner first. Read its metadata, methodology, release calendar, revision policy, and uncertainty documentation when they could affect interpretation.

Use an international harmonizer or aggregator only after checking whether it transforms, rebases, seasonally adjusts, converts, revises, or combines the source data. Use independent analysis for interpretation, not as a silent replacement for the underlying series.

## Verify numbers and calculations

Take values from the table or machine-readable series that defines the measure rather than from a chart, snippet, or article paraphrase. Preserve source precision and do not add meaningful digits through calculation.

For every derived result, retain the source operands, formula, direction, denominator, rounding rule, and resulting unit. Recalculate it independently. When the available tools cannot verify the arithmetic reliably, return the operands instead of asserting the derived value.

Use an exchange rate for the stated date or period and identify its source. Compare like definitions and vintages. If harmonization would require an unsupported assumption, report the values separately instead of manufacturing a ranking.

Check whether newer releases revised historical observations. Carry confidence intervals, sampling limits, methodology breaks, and forecast status when they could change the conclusion.

Also apply [news and current events](news.md) when the number is part of a new release, policy decision, company announcement, market-moving event, or contested interpretation. Apply [software documentation](software-documentation.md) when the result depends on a data API, package, query parameter, or machine-readable schema.
