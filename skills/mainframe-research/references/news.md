# News and current-events research

Apply this reference to events, announcements, disputes, allegations, investigations, incidents, and other public claims whose status can change with time.

## Separate time, fact, and attribution

Record when the event allegedly occurred, when each source first published, when it was last updated or corrected, and the research `as of` date. For recent developments, compare publication time with event time rather than treating the newest article as the newest event.

Distinguish confirmed fact, primary record, attributed report, allegation, interpretation, prediction, and unresolved claim. A statement proves that its source made the statement; it does not by itself prove that the statement is true.

## Establish source independence

Prefer evidence in this order:

1. Primary records such as official documents, filings, legislation, court records, transcripts, original datasets, direct statements, or authenticated original media.
2. Reporting with identifiable authorship, transparent corrections, and direct access to the event, records, or named sources.
3. Independent additional reporting that does not originate from the same wire story, press release, anonymous source, or social post.

Treat press releases and interested-party statements as primary evidence of that party's position only. Verify their external factual claims independently when those claims affect the answer. Several outlets repeating the same anonymous source or press release remain one evidence chain.

## Check the evolving record

Follow citations to the originating record. Check corrections, editor's notes, withdrawals, live-update timestamps, and material changes between versions. Do not use a repost, screenshot, aggregator, or search snippet when the originating material is accessible.

For images or video that materially affect the conclusion, establish the original publisher, capture time, location, and whether older media has been relabeled. If the available evidence cannot authenticate it, state that limitation.

For breaking news, prefer a smaller confirmed answer over a complete narrative assembled from unverified details. Do not treat absence of reporting as proof that an event did not occur.

Also apply [quantitative data](quantitative-data.md) to statistics, polls, prices, rates, budgets, company results, or calculated comparisons. Apply [software documentation](software-documentation.md) to technical releases, platform behavior, security advisories, or software incidents.
