# Experience

Use for portfolios, galleries, media showcases, interactive stories, and surfaces where exploration is itself the value.

- Choose a coherent visual idea that supports the subject rather than competing with it.
- Let imagery, type, rhythm, and transitions carry character while preserving understandable navigation.
- Make loading, media failure, captions, controls, focus, pause, and reduced-motion alternatives first-class states.
- Keep touch, keyboard, zoom, orientation, and performance constraints visible during design.
- Use progressive enhancement: the core content and path remain available when advanced effects fail.
- Verify the experience on a representative small screen and a constrained connection, not only a large desktop.

Avoid effects without narrative purpose, scroll hijacking, hidden exits, inaccessible canvases, and fragile animation chains.
