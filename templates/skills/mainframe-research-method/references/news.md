# News and current-events research

Apply this guide to events, announcements, disputes, developing stories,
investigations, incidents, and other claims whose truth can change with time.

## Separate time and attribution

- Record when the event allegedly happened, when each source first published,
  when it was last updated or corrected, and the research `as of` time.
- Attribute each disputed claim to the person or organization making it. A
  statement proves that the source said something, not that its content is true.
- Distinguish confirmed fact, attributed report, allegation, interpretation,
  prediction, and unresolved claim. Never turn one category into another.

## Source order and independence

1. Primary records: official documents, filings, court records, legislation,
   transcripts, original datasets, direct statements, or authenticated media.
2. Reporting by organizations with named authors, transparent corrections, and
   direct access to the event or sources.
3. Additional independent reporting that does not trace back to the same wire
   story, press release, anonymous source, or social-media post.

Treat press releases and interested-party statements as primary evidence of
their own position only. Vet their factual content independently. Prefer named
sources with direct knowledge. Anonymous sourcing is not independent
confirmation merely because several articles repeat it.

## Verification

- Follow citations to the originating record. Search snippets, aggregators,
  screenshots, reposts, and summaries are discovery aids, not final evidence.
- Check corrections, editor's notes, withdrawals, live-blog timestamps, and
  material changes between article versions.
- For photos or video material relevant to the conclusion, establish the
  original uploader, capture time, location, and whether older media has been
  relabeled. If available tools cannot authenticate it, state that limitation.
- For breaking news, prefer a smaller confirmed package over a fuller narrative
  built from unverified details. Name exactly what remains unconfirmed.
- Do not treat absence of reporting as evidence that an event did not occur.
